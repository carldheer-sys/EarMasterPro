Format and color conventions

  • Artist–title: Heading 2.

  • All other content: Normal text, 11 pt, 1.15 line spacing.

  • | separates measures. Both cells in a row always cover the same measures.

  • → marks a chord change within one measure.

  • – is the short pickup-bar marker when no chord sounds before the first entry.

  • - in the melody marks one contiguous rest interval reconstructed from note durations, whether or not Hooktheory stores an explicit rest event.

  • Inversions: the scale degree after / is the bass degree relative to the active key; for example, bVII/2 has scale degree 2 in the bass.

  • Extensions: suffixes such as 7, maj7, and 11 are written inline with the Roman numeral.

  • N.C. marks an explicit no-chord/rest span.

  • Melody: scale degrees use the catalog's fixed semitone-relative notation (e.g. b3, b6, b7 in a minor key), not Hooktheory's native scale-position numbers. In a minor key, the diatonic third/sixth/seventh are written b3, b6, b7; the chromatic major sixth is written 6 and is non-diatonic. In a major key, plain 3, 6, 7 are diatonic and b3, b6, b7 are non-diatonic.

  • Roman numerals: chords are relative to the active key and follow the catalog chord conventions.

  • Red melody degree or Roman numeral = non-diatonic note or chord.

  • Teal melody degree or Roman numeral = material after a key change within the same song part.

  • Color priority: red overrides teal when material after a key change is also non-diatonic.
